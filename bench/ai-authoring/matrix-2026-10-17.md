# The model matrix of 2026-10-17

The first run of `ai-authoring --matrix`: one model, six arms, six tasks, five
profiles, one trial per cell. The records are `runs/20261017-matrix-models/`
(`results.jsonl`, `summary.json`); the tables below are `ai-authoring --tables`
on that directory, not hand-copied.

## What ran

| | |
|---|---|
| Model | OpenCode Go `opencode-go/muse-spark-1.3-contributor`, closed-book |
| Commit | `cb0d7b4d2` (the merge of #595), the checkout every trial read its prompts and oracles from |
| Cells | 33 task-arm cells (three excluded: `recovery`/`old`, `capture`/`old`, `recovery`/`futures`, which have no sheet) times 5 profiles = 165 records |
| Trials per cell | 1. A second trial puts the same prompt twice, and the Director's order of 2026-10-17 allows no repetition in meaning |
| Billed | 4,569,097 tokens against a 9,000,000 budget (the Director's cap is 10M) |

The exit code is 1 because 14 records crashed (below). The router's
`exec-claude` route answered 410 ModelDeprecated and its `Executor` combo
answered empty, and Zen lists no muse model, so every call went through
OpenCode's Go muse.

No lower bound of a 95% Wilson interval passes 57 percent. The widest
difference, `capture`, is `futures` 0/5 [0, 43] against `fan` and `new` 4/5
[38, 96]: the intervals overlap, so no row separates two arms at 95%.

- `capture`: `futures` passed 0 of 5 and the `lgwks_bot` arms 2 to 4 of 5. The
  oracle fails a solution lacking the flood, orphan and cancel guarantees
  ([guarantees.md](guarantees.md)), which is what the `futures` reference lacks;
  the matrix does not read the five model solutions clause by clause.
- `tenant-cache` and `durable-retry`: all six arms passed 5 of 5, including
  `futures`, whose reference fails four of six clauses on `durable-retry` and
  two of six on `tenant-cache`. For these two tasks the matrix does not
  separate the arms, and why a model's `futures` solution passed where the
  reference fails is not measured here.
- `recovery`: `lexicon` passed 0 of 5, four of them crashed records.

## The 14 crashed records

`trial.rs` asked for a repair, the budget ledger refused it because it repeated
an earlier call in meaning, and the refusal propagated as an error. The runner
recorded `trial_id: "crashed"` with zero tokens, no compile verdict and no
repair count, which lost 1,196,329 tokens of real spend: the run billed
4,569,097 and its records sum to 3,372,768. They sit in `capture` (`futures`,
`lexicon`, `script`), `pipeline` (`new`, `script`) and `recovery` (`fan` 1,
`lexicon` 4, `new` 2, `script` 2). PR #596 makes a refused repeat end its trial
as itself, keeping the candidate, the repairs made and the tokens spent; the
14 are not resumed, because a resumed trial asks its first prompt again.

Two oracles also stalled the run by holding the one cargo lock, one spinning
and one parked, and were stopped by hand after 583 s and 386 s; their records
read 0/6 `not_run`.
